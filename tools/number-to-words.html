Why Nepal uses Lakh and Crore instead of Million and Billion

Nepal, like India, groups large numbers differently from the Western thousand/million/billion system. Instead of grouping every 3 digits, the Nepali numbering system groups the first 3 digits, then every 2 digits after that: 1 Lakh = 1,00,000 (100,000) and 1 Crore = 1,00,00,000 (10,000,000). This tool converts a plain number into written English words using that same Lakh/Crore grouping, so the wording matches what you'd write on a Nepali cheque, invoice, or loan document.

Common uses

  • Cheque writing: banks require the amount spelled out in words to prevent fraud.
  • Accounting and invoices: writing amounts in words alongside figures for legal documents.
  • Property and loan agreements: where amounts must be stated unambiguously in both numerals and words.
